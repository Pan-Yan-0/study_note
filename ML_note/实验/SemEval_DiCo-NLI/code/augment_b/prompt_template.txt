You are writing new training examples for a phrase-level natural language inference task.

TASK
Each example is an ordered pair of short English phrases (text1, text2). The phrases are chunks like those found in image captions or news headlines: noun phrases, verb phrases, prepositional phrases, or short headline fragments. The label gives the inference relation from text1 to text2:
- EQUIVALENCE: each phrase entails the other. Everything said in one phrase must also follow from the other, so neither phrase may add a place, time, participant, attribute or other detail that the other does not imply.
- FORWARD_ENTAILMENT: text1 entails text2, but text2 does not entail text1. Everything text2 says must follow from text1; text1 adds something more specific.
- NEGATIVE_OTHER: neither phrase entails the other. The two phrases should still be about a comparable part of a scene or story (for example a different place, attribute, number, action or participant), not random unrelated phrases.
Things that are related, likely or usually seen together do not count as entailment; only what must be true counts.
